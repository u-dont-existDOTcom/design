# Pattern Selection

Pattern selection happens after truth and perceptual requirements are established.

## Inputs

Surface mode and scope, audience job, L0–L4 requirements, incumbent identity, implementation boundaries, content shape, accessibility, performance, and verified guidance.

## Candidate construction

Generate two or three directions only when direction is open. Make them materially different in information architecture, hierarchy, navigation, composition, interaction, disclosure, or relationship between evidence and action. A color swap is not a new candidate.

Reject any unmet non-negotiable requirement before scoring.

## Automatic continuation

Continue when only one candidate survives, one has clearly superior requirement fit, the incumbent system determines direction, or the remainder is routine implementation detail. Ask only for a real surviving preference or strategic trade-off.

The policy kernel uses a default score margin of `0.12` after requirement filtering. Scores support judgment; they do not replace it.

## Catalog discipline

Search one dominant concern, verify domain/mode/stack/product fit, treat results as options, reject conflicts with higher authority, and do not persist unverified or zero-relevance output. Use checked-in guidance when optional upstream search is unavailable.

## Structural diversity

Avoid repeating generic rhythms across unrelated products. Inside one product, coherent repetition may be correct. Diversity is a defense against accidental templating, not a demand that every route look unrelated.
